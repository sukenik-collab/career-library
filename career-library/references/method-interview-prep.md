# Interview prep: story selection and speaking notes

Run this when the user asks for interview prep, which stories to tell,
STAR stories, or speaking notes for a role. It is the step after the CV:
pick the right stories for this interview, then produce a sheet the user
can glance at beside a video call.

## What to read first

- The posting. It is the lens for every decision below. If it isn't in
  the conversation, ask for it.
- If the CV for this role was built in this session, reuse that
  question-and-answer and the final CV. Don't interrogate from scratch.
- Otherwise read the library as in Step 0 of `method-cv.md`. The role
  files hold the story material.

## Rules

The CV rules do not relax for interviews.

- Never invent a fact, metric, stakeholder, or outcome. If it isn't in
  the user's telling or the library, mark it `[unconfirmed]`.
- Never upgrade authority. Carry the user's verbs.
- Honor every guard. In a live interview a guard is a landmine: the thing
  the user must not say under pressure. Collect the guards from each
  story's role file and from `guards.md`.
- Keep the user's voice.
- Do not skip the gates. Stop and wait at each one.

## Phase 1: Pick the stories

Select exactly three hero stories for this job. For each:

- The question themes it answers (ownership, conflict, ambiguity,
  impact).
- The project or projects it is built from.
- One line of rationale. Be opinionated. Where the user's obvious pick is
  the weaker choice for this role, say so and say why the better story
  wins.

Then a thin bench. For the common behavioral categories the three heroes
don't cover, name the single best-fit story in one line each. If a
category has no honest match in the library, say so and flag it to
develop. Do not manufacture one. A story that was really a
self-correction cannot answer a direct question about failure.

Heroes and bench together should cover: biggest impact; a failure or
mistake; conflict or disagreement, especially with someone senior;
ambiguity; influence without authority; getting work done through people
who didn't report to them; a decision driven by data or observation;
killing or changing their own idea; depth of user research.

**Gate:** present all three heroes and the bench together. Wait for the
user to confirm, swap, or add.

## Phase 2: Tighten each hero

For each confirmed hero:

1. Ask the user for a loose telling and any extra facts, however rough.
   Where the story needs it, and the entry's "What I'm proud of" or
   "What I couldn't do" is still blank, ask that now; closeout records
   the answer in the role file.
2. Tighten to situation, task, action, result. Situation short; it is
   setup. Action in "I," not "we." Result with the real number, in its
   original units.
3. Mark anything not in their telling or the library `[unconfirmed]`.

**Gate per story:** the user approves or corrects. Corrections are
re-tightened and gated again.

## Phase 3: Speaking notes per hero

- Three to five cue phrases: only what must land (a number, a turning
  point, the objection overcome). Noun phrases, not sentences.
- One line to open and one line to close. The close line is also the stop
  cue: once it's said, the story is over.
- Landmines: the guards that apply to this story, in the exact words the
  user should say.

Glanceable, not a script.

## Phase 4: Build the sheet

One self-contained page to park beside the call:

- The three heroes as prominent blocks: title and themes, cue phrases,
  open line, close line, landmine strip.
- The bench as a compressed strip at the bottom.
- A label with role, company, and date, so it doubles as a record.

Optimize for a glance: short cues, strong hierarchy, heroes large and
bench small. The open and close lines are the only full sentences on the
page. If `style.md` records the CV's colors, use them. Deliver it as a
file if you can produce one; otherwise as text in that order.

## Afterwards

Run `method-closeout.md`. Story tellings surface new facts and new
guards; those belong in the library.
