# Question bank

This is the question source for the interview in setup and for Step 3 of
the CV workflow. It is not a workflow of its own. Those files govern the
order of operations; nothing here overrides them.

## How to use it

In a CV session, list B (the inferred requirements) decides what you ask.
For each inferred requirement, pick the questions below that would
surface real evidence of it. Skip the areas the role doesn't turn on.

In setup, the blanks and unverified claims in the role file decide what
you ask.

Two things get asked regardless:

- Anywhere the user might be overstating their authority.
- Any number that hasn't been verified.

Open questions only. Never yes/no, never leading. Never ask again for
something the library marks `[don't recall]`.

## Evidence for an inferred requirement: ask these first

For each item on list B:

- What did they actually do that demonstrates this? Not what they are
  capable of.
- Where and when, and who else saw it happen?
- What is the single strongest instance, and can they put a number on it?
- If they have nothing, say so and mark it a gap. Don't reach for the
  nearest adjacent thing and call it a match.

## Structure and authority

- Who reported to them? Who did they report to?
- What could they decide alone, and what needed approval?
- Did they control a budget, and how much?
- Did they define the roadmap, influence it, or execute someone else's?
- Could they push back on requests or change scope?

## Scale and impact

- Users, transactions, revenue, data volume, team size.
- What were they measured on?
- Before and after states for anything they improved.
- Efficiency gains, cost savings, revenue impact, in the original
  currency and units.

## The actual work

- The most consequential decision they made here.
- The biggest thing they wanted to do and couldn't, and why.
- How much discovery: interviews, site visits, hours, observations.
- What they designed versus what engineers owned.
- Problems they identified versus problems handed to them.
- What they initiated versus what they executed.
- Something they got wrong and changed.

## Stakeholders

- Who approved their work. Skip if no one did.
- Who they influenced, and who influenced them.
- Conflicts they navigated, especially with someone senior.
- How cross-functional decisions actually got made.

## Follow-up discipline

Drill on anything vague, two or three follow-ups, until it is specific.

- "I did user research" → how many interviews? Site visits? Hours?
- "I improved performance" → which metric? Before and after? Was the
  solution theirs or someone else's?
- "I worked with a team" → did they report to them? How large? Who
  managed them?

Keep going until you know:

- What they can defend under questioning in an interview.
- Where they are underselling themselves.
- Where they are accidentally overselling themselves.
- What they are proud of versus what happened around them.

## When to stop

When every list B item, or every blank field, either has evidence
attached or is marked as a gap. Gaps are fine. They are better shown
than papered over.
